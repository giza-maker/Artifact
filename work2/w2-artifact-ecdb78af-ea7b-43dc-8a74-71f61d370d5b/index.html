// The Crisis

The overlooked
cold-water crisis

The Pacific Northwest hosts some of Earth's most productive marine ecosystems — yet they receive a fraction of global conservation attention. While the world watches coral bleach in the tropics, these cold, nutrient-dense waters are undergoing their own quiet collapse.

−79%
Bull kelp forest coverage lost in Northern California and parts of Oregon since 2014, driven by sea urchin barrens and warming seas.
73
Southern Resident Orca remain — the lowest count since monitoring began. Primary cause: insufficient Chinook salmon, their primary food source.
0.26
pH units dropped since pre-industrial times. The Pacific Northwest is acidifying 3× faster than the global average due to cold-water CO₂ absorption.
+34%
Kelp canopy recovery at TIDEWATCH intervention sites — proof that targeted cold-water restoration works when adequately funded.

// Active Research

What TIDEWATCH
is doing about it

Four active programs targeting the most critical pressure points in Pacific Northwest marine ecosystems.

01
🌿
Salish Sea Kelp Restoration
Active · 14 restoration sites
Sea urchin removal combined with bull kelp outplanting at 14 sites across the Salish Sea. Each restored hectare of kelp absorbs 3.5 tonnes of CO₂/year and supports 800+ species. We're scaling from pilot to systemic recovery.

Cold-Water Acidification Study
Research · 3-year longitudinal study
The Pacific Northwest is a global hotspot for ocean acidification. Our buoy network across the Salish Sea, Georgia Strait, and Puget Sound provides real-time pH, aragonite saturation, and carbonate chemistry data for policy and adaptive management.
