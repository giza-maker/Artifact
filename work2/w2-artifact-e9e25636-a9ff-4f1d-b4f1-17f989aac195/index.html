Step by Step

Your Hiring Roadmap

Five phases from "I want to work in games" to offer letter in hand. Each phase has clear deliverables.

01
Audit & Sharpen Your Portfolio
Studios hire on proof, not claims. You need 2–3 polished projects that demonstrate senior-level Unity skills: architecture decisions, performance optimization, and shipped product experience. Remove anything unfinished or from 5+ years ago.
GitHub polish Itch.io / Steam page GDD write-ups Video demos
02
Target the Right Studios
Match your genre experience to studio output. Mobile-casual, AAA, indie, and XR each have completely different tech stacks and culture. Mid-size studios (50–300 people) often give senior devs the most ownership and career growth.
LinkedIn Studio Alerts Games Industry Biz Remote-first studios
03
Network Before You Apply
67% of game dev jobs are filled through referrals. Go to GDC, attend online game jams, join studio Discord servers, and contribute to open-source Unity tools. A warm intro from an internal dev is worth 50 cold applications.
GDC / IGDA Game jams Twitter/X gamedev Discord communities
04
Nail the Interview Pipeline
Game studio interviews typically have 4 stages: recruiter screen → technical interview → take-home or live coding → system design + culture fit. Senior roles add a "how would you architect X?" round. Prepare a war chest of system design examples from your past work.
STAR method Live Unity demos Architecture whiteboard
05
Negotiate Total Comp
Game studios often underpay relative to tech. Use Levels.fyi and Glassdoor benchmarks. Always negotiate: base, bonus, royalties/profit share, equity, remote policy, and title. Your first number anchors the entire conversation — go 15–20% above your target floor.
Levels.fyi data Profit share Remote policy

Technical Leadership
Senior devs mentor juniors, lead code reviews, drive architectural decisions, and communicate trade-offs to non-technical stakeholders. Studios pay extra for this.
🤝
Cross-Discipline Collaboration
You'll work daily with artists, designers, and producers. Understanding their tools (Blender, Photoshop, JIRA) and constraints makes you invaluable.
🚀
Shipping Experience
Nothing beats shipped titles. Put every released game — indie, mobile, jam — on your CV. Studios look for developers who understand the full lifecycle to launch.
Show Your Work

Portfolio That Gets Interviews

Senior portfolios must demonstrate depth, not breadth. Three great projects beat twenty mediocre ones every time.

🏗️
Project 1 — Architecture Showcase
A project where you made significant design decisions. Document the "why" not just the "what".
  • System design doc (PDF / Notion)
  • Before/after performance metrics
  • Clean GitHub with README
  • 60-second video walkthrough
🎮
Project 2 — Shipped / Released Title
Any game that real players downloaded. Even a jam game with 200 plays shows you can ship.
  • Live store / Itch.io link
  • Your specific contribution list
  • Player count or download stats
  • Post-mortem write-up
🔧
Project 3 — Unity Tool / Package
A reusable Unity tool or editor extension shows senior-level thinking about developer experience.
  • Published to OpenUPM or GitHub
  • Documentation site
  • Stars / download count
  • Issue / PR history (activity!)

💡 Negotiate Everything
Title, remote days, royalties, signing bonus, equipment budget. Every element is negotiable at the senior level. Get competing offers if you can.
📊 Know Your Number
Research before every interview. Levels.fyi game-specific data, IGDA annual salary survey, and LinkedIn salary insights give you real leverage.
🏆 First 90 Days
Ship something small, fix something visible, build trust with your team lead. Propose architectural changes only after you understand the codebase.
