Philosophy

"These cars were never finished.
They were only interrupted." — Kaizen Motors Founding Principle
01 · Monozukuri
The Art of Making Things

Every build begins with the original engineer's intent. We study factory manuals, period development notes, and homologation documents before lifting a wrench.

02 · Kaizen
Continuous Improvement

Restomod isn't restoration alone. We improve every system — engine, suspension, brakes, electrics — to match what the original engineers would build today with modern materials.

03 · Ma
The Space Between

We reject the temptation to change everything. Some gaps are intentional. The best builds preserve the original's negative space — its character, its hesitations, its analog soul.

The Canadian Advantage

Why Vancouver
is the gateway
to JDM.

Canada's 15-year import rule — compared to the United States' 25-year threshold — makes Vancouver the most strategically positioned city in North America for accessing iconic Japanese platforms. Cars that remain federally prohibited south of the border are fully legal here today.

15
Year Canadian import rule — 10 years earlier than the US
R34
GT-R legal in Canada since 2014. US waits until 2024+
YVR
Direct port access to Japan — fastest in-transit times from Osaka and Yokohama
S15
Silvia, SW20, and other platforms available decades before US legalization

🇨🇦 Canadian Import Law — CBSA

Motor vehicles 15 years old or older are exempt from Transport Canada safety compliance requirements and can be imported freely for road use. This applies to all passenger vehicles regardless of origin country.

🇯🇵 Direct Japan Sourcing

Kaizen Motors maintains active relationships with auction houses in Osaka, Tokyo, and Nagoya — including USS, TAA, and JAA networks. We access grade 4+ single-owner vehicles before they reach the open market.

🌎 Global Delivery

Completed builds have been delivered to clients in the US (via collector car exemptions), UK, UAE, and Australia. We manage full export documentation, shipping logistics, and country-specific compliance review.

The Build Process

Every build is
a 12-month conversation.

We don't build cars in isolation. Every decision — from engine specification to seat leather — is made with the client before the first bolt is turned.

01
Phase One
Acquisition & Inspection

We source your vehicle from Japanese auction networks using our Osaka-based agent. Grade 4 or higher only. Full pre-purchase inspection, radiography of structural panels, and engine compression analysis before purchase. Car is imported directly to our Vancouver facility.

6 – 10 weeks
02
Phase Two
Teardown & Assessment

Complete disassembly. Every component is catalogued, measured, and assessed. Full structural inspection. Hidden rust, previous repairs, or non-factory modifications are documented and presented to the client with remediation options.

3 – 4 weeks
03
Phase Three
Fabrication & Build

The core work. Engine rebuild with specified components, suspension geometry correction, brake system overhaul, wiring loom replacement, body restoration to OEM dimensions. Weekly photographic reports delivered to client.

16 – 24 weeks
04
Phase Four
Refinement & Tuning

Dyno tuning, alignment, corner-balancing, and road calibration. We use Motec, Haltech, or original ECU depending on build intent. Fluid analysis after initial heat cycles. Suspension damper tuning session with client present.

4 – 6 weeks
05
Phase Five
Handover & Documentation

Full build documentation package: all receipts, component specifications, tuning maps, dyno sheets, and photographic record. White-glove delivery or professional shipping arranged. 2-year limited powertrain warranty on all builds.

1 – 2 weeks

The Atelier

A facility built
for obsessives.

Kaizen Motors operates from a 12,000 sq ft purpose-built facility in East Vancouver — 10 enclosed bays, a climate-controlled restoration cell, a full rotary rebuild room, and a 2,000 sq ft fabrication shop equipped with CNC porting, tube bending, and TIG/MIG welding capability.

Our team of nine combines factory-trained Japanese engineers, Canadian motorsport fabricators, and concours-level restoration specialists. Every Kaizen technician is certified to work on at least two of our five platform specializations.

We accept no more than six active builds simultaneously. This is a deliberate constraint. It ensures that every vehicle in the building receives the attention it deserves — and that our clients always know where their car stands.
